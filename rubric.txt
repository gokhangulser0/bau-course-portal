Problem 3/17: Force P Required to Break Contact at C

1. REFERENCE SOLUTION (FOR THE GRADER)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Given: The T-shaped body (m = 0.05 kg) is pinned at O. Take O as
the origin, x to the right, y up. Then:
  A = (-120, 0) mm   (P acts downward at A)
  B = (-60, -40) mm  (spring attached here, pulling left)
  C = (-60, -80) mm  (contact with the support on the left)
The center of gravity lies on the stem axis, 60 mm to the left
of O (by symmetry of the T).

Spring force:
  F_s = k * x = 1750 N/m * 0.010 m = 17.5 N, pulling B to the
  left (the spring is stretched). Its moment about O is clockwise.
  Moment arm = 40 mm (vertical distance from O to B).

Weight:
  W = m*g = 0.05 * 9.81 = 0.4905 N, downward, arm = 60 mm.
  Its moment about O is counterclockwise.

At the instant contact breaks, the contact force at C is zero.
So only P, W, the spring force and the pin reaction at O act on
the body. Taking moments about O eliminates the pin reaction.

(b) Neglecting weight (counterclockwise positive):
  Sum M_O = 0:   P(120) - 17.5(40) = 0
                 P(120) = 700 N*mm
                 P = 5.83 N

(a) Including weight:
  Sum M_O = 0:   P(120) + W(60) - 17.5(40) = 0
                 P(120) + 0.4905(60) - 700 = 0
                 P(120) = 700 - 29.43 = 670.57
                 P = 5.59 N

Final answers:
  (a) P = 5.59 N (including weight)
  (b) P = 5.83 N (neglecting weight)

3. GRADING POLICY (LENIENT - THIS IS A QUIZ)
------------------------------------------------------------------
1.  Error carried forward (ECF): If an early mistake is
    propagated consistently, do NOT penalize it again in later
    parts. Give full credit for later steps that are correct
    given the student's earlier values.

2.  Tolerance: Accept answers within +/-3% of the reference
    values (e.g., (a) P = 5.42-5.76 N, (b) P = 5.66-6.00 N).

3.  Value of g: g = 9.81, 9.8, or 10 m/s^2 are all acceptable.
    Do not penalize the small differences this causes in
    part (a).

4.  Unit slips: Mixing mm and m, or N and kN, costs at most
    3 points in total, as long as the numerical work is
    consistent. (A common slip is using x = 10 instead of
    0.010 m in F_s = k*x without converting units; treat it as
    a unit slip if the rest of the method is correct.)

5.  Sign / direction convention: If the student clearly states a
    different but reasonable sign convention (e.g., clockwise
    positive), grade against the student's own convention. Do
    not deduct more than 3 points in total for sign errors alone.

6.  Alternative methods: Taking moments about another point
    (for example C) is acceptable as long as the pin reaction
    at O is handled correctly. Using force equations in
    addition to moments is also acceptable.

7.  Minor arithmetic errors: A single arithmetic slip with
    correct method loses at most half of that line's points.

8.  Correct method, wrong or missing number: Award at least 50%
    of that line's points.

9.  Missing weight in part (a): If the student gives the same
    answer for (a) and (b) (forgets the weight in (a)), deduct
    only B2 and do not penalize B3 again (ECF).

10. Only one part answered: Grade the parts that are present
    normally. Do not penalize the missing part more than its
    own point allocation.

11. Partial work: Give credit for any correct, relevant step
    even if the student did not finish.

12. Presentation: Do not deduct for messy handwriting or a
    missing diagram beyond the 8 points in A1, or for unit
    formatting beyond the 10 points in Part D.

13. Blank or irrelevant answer: 0 points. Any genuine attempt
    with at least a correct setup should receive 15 points or
    more.
